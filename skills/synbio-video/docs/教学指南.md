# Teaching Guide

Audience: high-school students, introductory synthetic-biology learners, or teams making a project introduction for the first time. Students should be able to read a short science source; prior wet-lab work is not required.

## One 90-minute exercise

| Time | Student activity | What the teacher observes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–15 min | Read a teacher-provided research abstract with a clear citation and list two claims it supports | Whether students overstate a paper's findings as universal conclusions |
| 15–35 min | Turn one claim into a 30–45 second explanation; mark analogies and unknowns | Whether an analogy is mistaken for a real mechanism |
| 35–55 min | Plan shots and subtitles; label each visual “live footage / chart / AI illustration” | Whether charts have data sources and AI imagery is identifiable |
| 55–75 min | Use teacher-provided non-sensitive clips to fill a JSON manifest and run `--dry-run`; compose if possible | Whether students understand frame rate, duration, and source-audio removal |
| 75–90 min | Exchange reviews and name one clear expression and one claim with insufficient evidence | Whether feedback cites sources and visible evidence |

The class can begin by comparing a “goal statement” with an “experimental result statement” about the same concept. Ask students to write their own explanation before the AI drafts a full script; then compare content, sources, and omissions instead of simply copying an answer.

## Assessment rubric

Four dimensions, 0–2 points each: science claims match sources; explanation suits the audience; fact, goal, and illustration are distinguished; and deliverables play with their completion status stated accurately. 0 means missing or clearly incorrect, 1 means partly complete, and 2 means supported or checked adequately. A total of 6 or more may proceed to a rough-cut revision, but a score of 0 for science content must be corrected first; other points cannot compensate for it.

Teachers should review anything that could be mistaken for experimental instructions and check that viewers are not encouraged to imitate unsupervised procedures. Abstract workflows, colored blocks, engineering iteration diagrams, and public illustrations are suitable; real experiments are not required.

Submission may include the storyboard, source table, asset list, dry-run result or MP4/SRT, and two revision reasons written by the student. If video tools are unavailable, assess the script and storyboard and record “video not generated.”
