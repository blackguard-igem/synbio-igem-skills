# iGEM Mentor: Teaching Guide

## Goal

Learners should be able to locate official sources, decide whether a rule applies to their team, and use real evidence to explain project work. Sample answers support learning; they do not replace students' actual research or submissions.

## 30-minute rookie exercise: Find and explain a rule

This is a lesson plan designed by the package author.

- **5 min:** The teacher asks about a deliverable. Students write what they think the term means and what they are unsure about.
- **10 min:** With AI assistance, students locate the official page for the target year and record its section, link, and passage that supports the answer.
- **10 min:** Pairs exchange sources and check whether year, division, or exceptions were omitted.
- **5 min:** Students explain the conclusion in Chinese and write a two-sentence English summary. AI checks whether conditions match.

Assess whether students found an accessible source, can identify the relevant clause, and can distinguish a rule from a team goal or recommendation. Do not grade them on matching the AI's wording.

## Guidance by project phase

During topic selection, discuss needs and evidence. During implementation, record design, measurement, and learning. During documentation, map materials to official requirements. For presentations, rehearse with the team's real data. Offer the most useful next step for the current phase and record matters requiring mentor confirmation.

The teacher can ask the AI to prompt students: “Which part of your work supports this claim?”, “What decision changed?”, or “What did not work?” After the student responds, give one specific strength and one concrete gap before offering an example if needed.

## Different roles

Rookies practice terminology and source-finding; experienced teams verify evidence against current-year changes; teachers design learning tasks and team roles; school administrators receive concise eligibility, fee, and timeline explanations with unverified items kept visible.

## Evidence and integrity

Label classroom simulations as “teaching simulation.” AI must not invent interviews, experiments, completed work, or member contributions. Preserve students' original work and identify AI-assisted content. Safety learning focuses on finding applicable policies, information gaps, and responsible decision-makers; the AI does not grant approval.
