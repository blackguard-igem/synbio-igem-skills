# Teaching Guide

## Learning goals

Learners should be able to explain a biological system's input, mechanism, and output; distinguish evidence from inference; and ask a question that could change their judgment. Match the task to learners' prior knowledge rather than assuming ability from age alone.

## Three levels

| Level | Learning activity | Observable outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introductory | Explain a concept and identify where an analogy works and fails | Explain in their own words and answer a transfer question |
| Intermediate | Compare figures with a paper's conclusions; find controls and limitations | One-page evidence note with a location for each claim |
| Project | Compare two candidate approaches and their measurable outcomes | Explain the choice, evidence gaps, and next step |

## Sample 45-minute lesson: What evidence supports a scientific claim?

This lesson is an instructional example designed by the package author.

1. **5 min:** Students separately explain “the system can work” and “the system is suitable for real-world use.” Record their initial answers.
2. **10 min:** Choose a topic from the Chinese expert profiles. Show an official profile or paper excerpt that was actually accessed and identify the source type.
3. **10 min:** Students classify three statements as established facts, interpretations, or untested hypotheses, and explain why.
4. **10 min:** A peer proposes a changed condition; students decide whether the original conclusion can be generalized.
5. **10 min:** Students revise an overstatement and specify what additional evidence is needed. Let the AI offer a hint first, then show one sample analysis.

## Prompts and feedback

First ask students to state their understanding or reasoning. If they are stuck, offer a relevant concept and one clue. If needed, demonstrate a similar problem and then ask them to solve the original. When a user explicitly requests a worked answer, provide it and explain the reasoning.

Assess whether the mechanism is plausible, evidence matches the claim, limitations are acknowledged, and learning transfers. Record “demonstrated / needs more evidence / not yet observed” with a concrete example. Do not equate fluent answers with understanding or invent learning-gain percentages.

## Teacher and AI roles

The teacher selects reliable materials, adjusts difficulty, and checks scientific content; the AI explains, asks follow-up questions, and organizes information. Use institution-approved conditions and procedures for lab classes. Expert profiles are for comparing public research approaches, not substitutes for evidence or teacher judgment.
